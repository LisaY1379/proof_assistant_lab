## Project Title
Bridging Neural Networks and Formal Logic (AI4math) – Phase 2: Enhanced Agent Ability in Mathematical Discovery and Proof Representation with Lean Math Library
## Proposed Study
This research is a continuation of the project conducted during the summer independent study. We tested agent-assisted search for Pomerance Triples during research phase 1, and we discovered that the logical representation ability of agentic AI is proficient in data-driven and engineering algorithm optimization, but limitations occur when an original mathematical discovery is needed. At the same time, we realized that communication frictions occur during the interaction process with an agent due to it lacking an intrinsic awareness of the significance of each logical step and the user’s knowledge base. However, it is hypothesized that a formalized model has the potential to address both problems since their common cause is lacking explicit logical structure. In phase 2, we will focus on improving the agent’s ability in finding new discovery utilizing the Lean library framework. We will also focus on the agent’s ability in representing its proofs, aiming for a better understandability and efficient communication in human interactions.
## Research Methodology
We will make use of the agentic pipeline created during phase 1 – but upgrade it with a logical backbone: we will import the Lean Library as the knowledge database and create an explicit database of proof strategies, and the agent will search and call the exact theorem and proof it want to use whenever it drafts new proofs and explanations to the user. We will first start it locally on the field of real analysis, finish the building of the proof strategy dataset and the logical backbone library. Then, we will test the quality of its outputs with and without the backbone library. The outputs are evaluated by understandability and possibly requires human tests and feedback for evaluation. Depending on the progress, we will probably introduce: (1) Scaling into other fields of mathematics and test for generalizability; (2) Personalized user profile; (3)Introduce a more formalized directed acyclic graph (DAG) model representing the structure of math knowledge base and string diagrams representing logical relations, embed it into current library and tests for improvements.

## Course Outcomes
This phase of research will provide an evaluation of the usefulness and limitations of current agentic tools and pipelines, as well as the proof of concept of the formalized Lean Mathlib model on a concrete mathematical field. We will record the experiment details and results in both stages, as well as the rigorous definition and implementation of the Lean Mathlib model.
