Hanson–Wright

How far can a random quadratic quantity stray from its average?

THE DESTINATION
ℙ(∣XᵀAX − 𝔼XᵀAX∣ ≥ t)
≤ 2 exp[−c min{t²/V, t/L}]
V = K⁴‖A‖F² · L = K²‖A‖ · Independent, centered subgaussian Xᵢ

THE QUESTION

Random inputs. A predictable total?

Imagine independent sources of noise. You combine their values using squares and products, then ask: how likely is the result to be far from its average?

Hanson–Wright gives an upper bound on that probability. It applies when individual inputs have sufficiently light tails, and it describes how the size and structure of the interaction matrix affect the bound.

The idea to keep: control of individual inputs can give control of a quadratic quantity, even though the products inside it may depend on one another.
A CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Total noise energy

Let X₁, …, Xₙ be independent, mean-zero noise values, each with variance 1 (so each squared value has average 1). If A is the identity matrix—ones on the diagonal and zeros elsewhere—the expression is simply:

XᵀAX = X₁² + ⋯ + Xₙ²

Its average is n. The theorem bounds the chance that this total differs from n by at least t, assuming the noise values are subgaussian.

Beyond squares: a general matrix also weights interactions such as X₁X₂. The products X₁X₂ and X₁X₃ share an input, so they need not be independent. This is the difficulty behind the decoupling step.

01 · Read the statement: objects, symbols & assumptions

Read the inequality as: “The probability that the quadratic expression differs from its average by at least t is no larger than this exponentially decreasing bound.”

X and A · inputs and weights
X = (X₁, …, Xₙ) is a vector of real random inputs. A is a fixed real n × n matrix. It weights their squares and pairwise products; it need not be positive or symmetric.
XᵀAX · a quadratic form
The compact matrix notation means ∑ᵢ ∑ⱼ aᵢⱼXᵢXⱼ. The superscript T means transpose. Diagonal entries multiply squares Xᵢ²; off-diagonal entries multiply different coordinates XᵢXⱼ.
𝔼 and ℙ · average and probability
𝔼XᵀAX is the average over repeated draws of X. ℙ(…) is the probability of the event inside the parentheses. The absolute value includes deviations both above and below the average.
Independent and centered
The coordinates are jointly independent, and each has mean zero: 𝔼Xᵢ = 0. Zero correlation alone is not the independence assumption. The coordinates need not have identical distributions or variance 1.
Subgaussian and K · light tails
“Subgaussian” means large magnitudes become unlikely at least as fast as a Gaussian-type bound, up to a scale factor. Gaussian and bounded random variables are examples. Here K bounds every coordinate’s subgaussian norm; larger K allows a weaker concentration bound.
t and c · threshold and constant
You choose t ≥ 0, the deviation size of interest. The positive constant c is universal: it does not depend on n, A or t. This statement does not specify a numerical value for c, so it is not a calibrated probability calculator.
‖A‖F · all entries together
The Frobenius norm is √(∑ᵢⱼ aᵢⱼ²). It measures the total squared size of the matrix entries.
‖A‖ · the strongest direction
The operator norm is max‖v‖₂=1 ‖Av‖₂. It measures the largest stretch of a unit vector. Here ‖v‖₂ = √(∑ᵢ vᵢ²) is ordinary Euclidean length.
What exactly is the subgaussian norm ‖Y‖ψ₂?

One standard convention is ‖Y‖ψ₂ = inf{s > 0 : 𝔼 exp(Y²/s²) ≤ 2}. It measures a scale for the tail of Y, rather than just its variance. Equivalent conventions change only universal constants. In this explorer, K = maxᵢ ‖Xᵢ‖ψ₂.

Scope: A is fixed, and the coordinates are independent, centered and subgaussian. The displayed result is not a general bound for arbitrary dependent or heavy-tailed inputs.
02 · Why are there two norms and a minimum?

The exponent uses two scales: V = K⁴‖A‖F² and L = K²‖A‖. The minimum chooses the smaller exponent, giving the weaker of the two exponential bounds.

Moderate deviations

t²/V

For t ≤ V/L, this is the smaller term. The exponent is quadratic in the deviation, and the Frobenius norm controls its scale.

Larger deviations

t/L

For t ≥ V/L, this is the smaller term. The exponent grows linearly, and the operator norm controls its scale.

These regimes describe the bound; the theorem does not claim an exact probability distribution. When the right side exceeds 1, the probability bound is uninformative. The crossover assumes A ≠ 0 and K > 0; if either is zero, the centered quadratic quantity is zero.

03 · Where does this show up, and why is it useful?

Energy after a linear transformation. If a fixed matrix B transforms noise X, then its squared output length is ‖BX‖₂² = XᵀBᵀBX. Hanson–Wright applies with A = BᵀB. This turns a question about a random vector’s length into a quadratic-form bound.

Random projections. Squared distance to a fixed subspace can also be written as a quadratic form, using the orthogonal projection onto its complement. The theorem controls fluctuations of this distance squared around its mean.

The useful feature is that the estimate retains both matrix norms. Treating every product separately with a crude bound can lose that structure and introduce unnecessary dependence on the number of coordinates.

Further reading: Rudelson & Vershynin, Hanson–Wright inequality and sub-gaussian concentration, a modern proof with applications to random vectors and matrices.

04 · Enter the proof: prerequisites & a quick tour

You can start with expectation, independence, matrix multiplication and Euclidean length. The more specialized tools are explained when they enter the written argument.

Tail bound
An upper bound on how likely a large deviation is. The theorem’s conclusion is a two-sided tail bound.
Exponential moment / MGF
The expectation 𝔼 exp(λY). Exponentiation makes large positive values count heavily. Bounding this quantity lets Markov’s inequality turn it into a tail bound.
Decoupling
A comparison that introduces an independent copy X′, so one factor can be held fixed while the other remains random. It is an inequality, not a claim that the old and new expressions have the same distribution.
Gaussian replacement
A comparison of exponential moments with Gaussian inputs. Gaussian rotation properties then make the matrix calculation easier.
